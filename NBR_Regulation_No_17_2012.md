Chapter I Scope of Application

### Article 1

(1) For the purpose of maintaining financial stability, this regulation establishes certain conditions for granting and servicing loans in Romania to natural persons and non-financial entities and applies to the following categories of entities, hereinafter referred to as lenders:

a) Romanian credit institutions that are legal persons, and branches of foreign credit institutions;

b) Romanian non-bank financial institutions that are legal persons, and branches of foreign financial institutions, registered in the General Register;

c) Romanian payment institutions that are legal persons; and

d) Romanian electronic money issuers that are legal persons.

### Article 2

(1) Institutions referred to in Article 1(3) that conduct foreign-currency lending activities in other member states for debtors exposed to foreign-exchange risk and domiciled or having their registered office in those member states must comply, in respect of those activities, with the relevant requirements imposed by the competent authorities of the member states concerned, as communicated by the National Bank of Romania. (2) For the purpose of applying paragraph (1), the National Bank of Romania publishes on its website the requirements communicated by the competent authorities of the respective member states.
### Article 3
For the purposes of this regulation, the following terms and expressions have the meanings set out below:

a) real estate investment loan - any loan granted to a natural person who cumulatively meets the following conditions: (i) is secured by a mortgage on real estate and (ii) is granted for the purpose of acquiring or maintaining property rights over a plot of land and/or a building, completed or to be completed, or for the purpose of rehabilitation, modernization, consolidation or extension of a building or for the development of a plot of land or exclusively for the purpose of repaying a real estate investment loan. For the purpose of this regulation, real estate finance leases granted to natural persons are assimilated to real estate investment loans;

b) consumer credit - a credit granted to a natural person, other than the credit provided for in letter (a);

c) credit - the amount recorded in balance-sheet or off-balance-sheet accounts, made available or that the lending institution has undertaken to make available to a natural person or non-financial entity, in exchange for the obligation of the debtor concerned to repay that amount and to pay interest or other expenses related to it; finance-lease financing is treated as credit;

d) person not naturally hedged against foreign-exchange risk - a natural person who does not obtain eligible income for repaying the credit, or a non-financial entity that does not have sources for repaying the credit, denominated in or indexed to the currency in which the credit is requested or granted;

e) non-financial entities - legal persons, natural persons acting for purposes related to their commercial or professional activity and any other categories of entities, whose main activity is outside the activities included in the financial intermediation and insurance sector, according to the Classification of Activities in the National Economy.

f) total debt-to-income ratio - an indicator that reflects the total indebtedness of a debtor, determined in accordance with Article 13.

Chapter II Common Provisions

### Article 6

(1) Lenders conduct lending activities for natural persons and non-financial entities on the basis of their own regulations, approved by the competent statutory bodies. (2) To demonstrate compliance with the requirements imposed by this regulation, lenders shall submit their own regulations, in Romanian, to the Supervision Directorate of the National Bank of Romania, both electronically and on paper, accompanied by the decision of the competent statutory body that approved them and by a statement of reasons.

### Article 7

(1) Lenders, as well as the institutions referred to in Article 1(2), must inform persons not naturally hedged against foreign-exchange risk who apply for a loan in foreign currency, or indexed to the exchange rate of a foreign currency, of the impact that a possible severe depreciation of the national currency would have on their periodic payment obligations. (2) In addition to the information provided under paragraph (1), for loans without a fixed interest rate, lenders and the institutions referred to in Article 1(2) must separately present the level that those periodic payment obligations could reach as a result of a possible severe depreciation of the national currency combined with an increase in interest rates.

### Article 8 Lenders shall provide the information required under Article 7(1) and, where applicable, paragraph (2), tailored to each loan application, so that the periodic payment obligation for the requested financing, determined using the exchange rate and interest rate applicable on the date the information is provided, can be compared with the estimated periodic payment obligation determined in accordance with Article 7(1) or, as applicable, paragraph (2).

### Article 9

(1) Lenders, as well as the institutions referred to in Article 1(2), provide the information set out in Article 7 on paper or another durable medium, at least 15 calendar days before the conclusion of the credit agreement. (2) The 15-calendar-day period set out in paragraph (1) may be reduced at the written request of the credit applicant. (3) For the purposes of paragraph (1), a durable medium means any instrument that allows the credit applicant to store information addressed personally to them in such a way that it can be accessed for future consultation for a period of time appropriate to the purpose of the information, and that allows the faithful reproduction of the stored information.

### Article 10 Lenders must organize their lending activities in such a way as to ensure the clear and effective separation of the function of promoting and selling credit products from the function of credit risk analysis and monitoring of exposure.

### Article 11

(1) Lenders must ensure that third parties involved in the preliminary analysis of the client’s repayment capacity, such as credit intermediaries, apply the same procedures as lenders for this purpose. (2) Lenders monitor the quality of the loan portfolio related to clients attracted by each third party referred to in paragraph (1), compared to that of the loan portfolio related to clients directly attracted by lenders. (3) The analysis carried out in accordance with paragraph (2) forms the basis of the lender’s decision to continue the business relationship with each of the third parties referred to in paragraph (1).

### Article 11^4

(1) When assessing clients' repayment capacity, lenders shall take into account the income available at the time of the credit application, its history and development over time, the level and nature of clients' expenses, and any other financial and economic factors considered necessary, sufficient, and proportionate for that purpose. (2) When assessing a client's repayment capacity, lenders shall give primary consideration to the client's ability to generate the income needed to repay the credit and only secondary consideration to the fact that the collateral value exceeds the credit amount or to the prospect of an increase in the value of residential immovable property where the credit is granted to construct or renovate that property. (3) Lenders shall obtain the information referred to in paragraph (1) from clients and other relevant internal or external sources, including, where applicable, by consulting the database of a credit-information system, such as a credit bureau, and from credit intermediaries involved in activities preliminary to the conclusion of credit agreements. (4) Lenders shall take reasonable steps to verify the information obtained, including by consulting independent sources where appropriate.

### Article 11^5

(1) Lenders shall establish and apply remuneration policies for their staff and for credit intermediaries acting on their behalf in a manner that ensures that, when developing credit products, granting or brokering loans, providing ancillary services, and recovering claims arising from loans, they act honestly, fairly, transparently, and professionally, taking into account clients' rights and interests. (2) When establishing and applying remuneration policies for staff responsible for assessing clients' repayment capacity, lenders must observe, in a manner and to an extent appropriate to their size and internal organization and to the nature, scope, and complexity of their activities, the following principles:

a) the remuneration policy promotes and is compatible with sound and effective risk management, without encouraging the assumption of risks that exceed the lender's risk tolerance level;

b) the remuneration policy corresponds to the lender's business strategy, objectives, values, and long-term interests and includes measures to avoid conflicts of interest, including by ensuring that remuneration does not depend on the number or proportion of loan applications accepted. (3) Romanian credit institutions that are legal persons shall apply paragraphs (1) and (2) when establishing and applying remuneration policies pursuant to National Bank of Romania Regulation No. 5/2013 on prudential requirements for credit institutions, as subsequently amended and supplemented.

Chapter IV Provisions on foreign currency financing of non-financial entities

### Article 21

(1) Lenders establish within their own regulations, in line with their risk profile and strategy, the manner of organizing the activity of granting and guaranteeing foreign currency loans to non-financial entities, imposing stricter conditions for granting and guaranteeing foreign currency loans than those applicable to similar loans in national currency. (2) The lenders’ own regulations regarding foreign currency financing of non-financial entities, referred to in this chapter, constitute a single internal regulation.

### Article 22

(1) Lenders may grant loans in foreign currency, or indexed to the exchange rate of a foreign currency, to non-financial entities only where, according to the criteria established by the lender in its own regulations, those entities demonstrate that they can repay the foreign-currency financing even in the event of a severe depreciation of the currency in which their repayment sources are denominated or to which those sources are indexed and, where applicable, an increase in the interest rate. (2) Repealed.

### Article 23

(1) This chapter applies only to the portion of financing for which the debtor does not demonstrate natural or financial hedging against foreign-exchange risk. (2) For the purposes of paragraph (1), financial hedging against foreign-exchange risk means protection obtained by the debtor under a contract with a financial institution through which foreign-exchange risk is transferred from the purchaser of the protection. (3) The provisions concerning interest-rate risk do not apply to fixed-interest-rate loans.
